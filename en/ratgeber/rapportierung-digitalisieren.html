From Excel and paper slips to digital job reporting — without stopping the business

The software is rarely the problem. The rollout is what fails. A realistic path over four weeks.

Updated 2026-09-04 · 5 minute read

Most businesses do not fail at choosing software. They fail at rolling it out: the licence is running, but half the team still writes on paper, and after three months you have both — the old mess plus an invoice.

Why Excel worked for so long

Excel is not stupid. It is there instantly, costs nothing extra, and any process can be modelled in it. The price only shows up later: the file lives with one person, two people cannot edit it at once, on a phone it is unusable, and nobody notices when a formula has been calculating wrongly for months.

The point to switch has arrived when gathering the data takes longer than the work being documented.

A realistic timeline

Week 1 — hours only, one crew only

Not everything at once. One crew, one function: book hours to the job. Nothing else. Anyone who converts materials, quotes and invoices in the same first week creates resistance that never goes away.

Week 2 — run both in parallel

Keep the paper slips and capture digitally as well. It feels like double work, and it is — but it is the only week in which mistakes surface without a wrong invoice going out.

Week 3 — switch the paper off

Now without exceptions: no more slips accepted. Anyone who accepts them keeps the old system alive. That single decision determines the whole migration.

Week 4 — the invoice out of the data

Only once capture is solid comes the second step: generating the invoice directly from the recorded hours. That is the moment the benefit becomes visible to everyone — and after which nobody wants to go back.

The most common mistake is starting with the longest-serving, most sceptical people "to see whether it works". Better the other way round: start with those who find it practical anyway. If they use it, the rest follow on their own.

What to think about beforehand

  • Does capture work offline? In a basement or a tunnel there is no signal.
  • Can people manage without training? If not, it is too complicated.
  • Where does the data sit? Switzerland or the EU saves the later discussion.
  • Can you get out again? Exporting your own data should be a given.
  • What does it cost per person per month — and what happens when fewer people work in winter?

What the switch realistically delivers

No software turns a disorganised business into an organised one. What it reliably changes is the distance between the work and the invoice. Businesses that used to spend two evenings a month sorting slips find the invoice goes out the day after the job. The money arrives correspondingly earlier — and for a small business that often matters more than any hour saved.
